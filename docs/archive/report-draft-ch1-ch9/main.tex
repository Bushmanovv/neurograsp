\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Brain--Computer Interface (\acrshort{bci}) technology enables direct
communication between the human user and external devices, bypassing
damaged neuromuscular pathways. This project presents the design,
implementation, and validation of a real-time \acrshort{eeg}-based
\acrshort{bci} system for controlling a 3D-printed prosthetic hand
using voluntary physiological artifact signals — specifically, eye
blinks and jaw muscle activations (clinching and lateral bruxism).

Unlike conventional motor imagery approaches that rely on subtle
sensorimotor rhythm modulations, the proposed system exploits the
large-amplitude, highly distinctive artifacts captured by a scalp
\acrshort{eeg} montage: ocular potentials (\acrshort{eog}) from
voluntary blinks at the frontal electrodes, and muscle potentials
(\acrshort{emg}) from jaw activation at the temporal electrodes. A
19-channel \acrshort{eeg} device recording at \SI{200}{\hertz} in
European Data Format (\acrshort{edf}) captures five distinct voluntary
commands: single blink (open hand), double blink (start/confirm), jaw
clinch (close hand), left bruxism (rotate left), and right bruxism
(rotate right).

The processing pipeline encompasses notch and bandpass filtering,
Common Average Reference (\acrshort{car}) spatial normalisation, and
activity-gated epoch extraction. The central methodological
contribution is a \textbf{three-way hybrid classification
architecture} in which each gesture family is routed to the method
best matched to its underlying physiology: hand-crafted channel-aware
features perform the initial blink-versus-muscle separation, Dynamic
Time Warping (\acrshort{dtw}) shape-matching sub-classifies the blink
commands, and Riemannian covariance geometry sub-classifies the muscle
commands. Evaluated under the strict, cross-session
Leave-One-Session-Out (\acrshort{loso}) protocol --- which eliminates
the epoch-overlap data leakage that inflates naive estimates --- the
final system achieves \textbf{91.3\%} overall accuracy with a
macro-averaged F1-score of \textbf{0.913}, improving on a flat
Random Forest baseline (85.1\%) through the hierarchical routing,
Riemannian muscle classification (+4.1\%), and \acrshort{dtw} blink
classification (+2.1\%). Real-time inference employs a sliding window
of \SI{2.0}{\second} with \SI{0.2}{\second} step, a confidence
threshold of 0.60, and a Finite State Machine (\acrshort{fsm})
requiring three consecutive identical predictions before issuing a
command to the Arduino-controlled prosthetic hand, at a per-window
latency of \SI{14}{\milli\second}.

The system demonstrates that voluntary artifact-based control is a
practical, robust, and accessible alternative to motor imagery for
users with severe upper-limb amputations, offering clear,
large-amplitude signals that are easier to classify reliably under
real-world, cross-session conditions.
